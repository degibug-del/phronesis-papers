\begin{abstract}
Art has two ground states: expression (what the artist intends to say) and commodity (what the market will buy). When art is made for sale, the artist displaces from the first ground state to the second. Not through corruption but through structural cost: the path to commodity is cheaper than the path to pure expression. The tension between these attractors is the condition of art.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Two Grounds}

Expression ground state $S_0$: the artist makes what they need to make, regardless of market.

Commodity ground state $S^*$: the artist makes what sells, regardless of intent.

Both are stable. Both are internally coherent. The cost of maintaining each varies.

\section{Why Markets Drive Displacement}

An artist can survive on expression alone only if:
\begin{enumerate}
\item They have resources (family money, day job, patronage)
\item Their expression accidentally aligns with market demand
\item They are willing to suffer material deprivation
\end{enumerate}

Most artists have none of these. The cost to reach $S^*$ (make sellable work) is lower than the cost to remain at $S_0$ (make only what you believe in, without income).

The system works perfectly. The artist returns to the market-attractive state.

\section{The Attractor of Aesthetics}

Once in the commodity state, the artist learns what sells: certain styles, certain subjects, certain emotional tones. These become the new ground. The artist has not sold out. They have found a stable state and optimized within it.

Breaking free requires discontinuity: refusing the market, accepting deprivation, or finding a new patron structure (grants, residencies, teaching, cryptocurrency).

The original ground (pure expression) is still available. But the return cost is prohibitive.
