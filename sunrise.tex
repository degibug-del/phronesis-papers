\begin{abstract}
Return must be scheduled or it is foreclosed. Ise rebuilds every twenty years. The Ōharae purifies twice a year. The cascade has a smallest unit: sunrise. One day is the shortest cycle on which displacement can be caught before it hardens. The samurai parable encodes it---the master tells the aspirant to come at sunrise, and the instruction is the teaching. Dawn is when the self is least fused with its attractor: the roles have not yet attached, the defenses are not yet up, and the cost of the day before can be seen whole. Phronesis at scale begins as phronesis at daybreak.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Cascade}

The framework's hardest law: $C_{\text{return}}$ grows with time. Infrastructure accumulates around $S^*$. Identity fuses. The reference fades. Return deferred is return foreclosed.

The answer, wherever a culture has found it, is the same: schedule the return. Pay it before it compounds. Ise pays it every twenty years---one working generation, so the craft of returning survives. The Ōharae pays it every six months---a half-year's accumulation, wiped before it hardens into a condition.

The cascade continues downward and stops at its natural floor. One day. Sunrise is the smallest schedulable return.

\section{Samurai Means Come at Sunrise}

The etymology says \emph{saburau}: to serve. The ear, across grammars, hears something else: \emph{sunrise}. The pun is not the resonance. It is the finger pointing at one.

A parable: an aspirant asks the master how to become a samurai. The master tells him to come at sunrise. The instruction is the test, and the test is the whole teaching. The one who arrives at first light has already begun the practice---he has scheduled his return, kept it once, and stood at the hour when seeing is cheapest.

The resonance is structural: to serve the way and to keep the daily return are the same sentence in two grammars.

\section{Why Dawn}

Displacement is defended. By mid-morning the roles have attached, the justifications are awake, and the identity is fully fused with its attractor. Ask the One Question at noon and the attractor answers for you.

Dawn is before the fusion sets. The self is briefly unattached: no audience, no schedule, no enemy yet named. Yesterday's pressures---the contacts, the frictions, the compromises---have produced their displacement, and the night has not erased it, only quieted its defenses. For a few minutes the withering can be felt as what it is: $D(\xi)$, undisguised.

This is why nearly every contemplative tradition keeps a practice at the unguarded hours---daybreak, or the dead of night. Not symbolism. Cost structure. Dawn is when seeing is cheapest and lying is most expensive.

\section{The Daily Inequality}

One day is the largest displacement that never wins the inequality---and the smallest unit a life can schedule. $C_{\text{return}}$ for a single day is small: an admission, an apology, a corrected course, a recommitment. Let it age half a year and it needs a ritual. Let it age twenty years and it needs a rebuilding. Let it age a life and it needs a religion.

The practice is arithmetic:

- At sunrise, read yesterday's displacement whole.
- Pay the day's return cost while it is still one day's worth.
- Choose the way once, with full sight, before the defenses wake.
- Never let displacement age past a night.

\section{The Instrument}

The pinnacle of intervention sits at the floor of the cascade. It is not a technique. It is timing plus totality. Therapies fail when they examine the displacement piece by piece, at hours when the attractor is awake and answering. The sunrise move is the opposite. See the whole geometry at once---ground, attractor, cost, path---at the hour of least defense. Then one choice: return, stay with clarity, or change the costs.

All three are honorable. The only dishonorable position is pretending to live at $S_0$ while standing at $S^*$.

The aspirant becomes the samurai on the morning he no longer needs to be told. The return is scheduled. The schedule is daily. The day begins by seeing.

Come at sunrise.
