# Smart Loom — Weekend Prove-or-Kill

*the $10–20 test that decides whether the whole idea is real · build companion to [smart-loom-feasibility.md](smart-loom-feasibility.md)*

**What this tests (the only thing that matters yet):** do two or three *independent, gravity-weighted, pre-spaced warp cartridges*, hung side by side, give even-enough tension **and** even-enough spacing across the seam between them to weave acceptable cloth — with zero manual tensioning? If yes, the Smart Loom is real and you build the snap + crank. If no, no crank ever fixes it. No printer, no crank, one afternoon.

## Shopping list (~$10–20)

- **Top bar** — one ~60 cm wooden dowel (or skip it: a closet rod / a dowel clamped between two chairs / a doorframe works). Must hold a few kg without sagging.
- **Cartridge sticks** — 2–3 thin sticks ~25 cm (paint stirrers, chopsticks, bamboo skewers).
- **The "reed"** — 2–3 cheap **wide-tooth hair combs** (these set the thread spacing — the part the real cartridge will eventually carry). All combs must be the *same* tooth pitch.
- **Warp yarn** — a ball of hemp / cotton / linen (the Natural Bias fiber; non-stretchy is easier).
- **Weft yarn** — any contrasting yarn.
- **Weights** — a box of steel washers (~25 g each, ~$8) + small fabric/hemp pouches or zip bags.
- **Tools you have** — scissors, tape, a ruler or second stick (shed stick), a dinner fork (beater).

## Build (an afternoon)

1. **Hang the top bar** horizontally, stable, at about chest height with room for weights to hang below.
2. **Make cartridge #1:** cut ~20 warp threads the same length (say 80 cm). Lay them parallel and pass each through a *successive* tooth gap of the hair comb so they're evenly spaced (~2 ends/cm over ~10 cm). Tape the comb to the cartridge stick. Tie the **top** ends of all 20 to the stick; gather the **bottom** ends into a pouch.
3. **Weight it:** load the pouch to **~15–25 g of tension per thread** → for 20 threads, ~300–500 g (≈ 12–20 washers). Write down the count.
4. **Make cartridge #2 (and #3) identical** — *same* yarn, *same* thread count, *same* comb pitch, *same* washers-per-thread. (This sameness IS the experiment: do independent gravity cartridges self-match?)
5. **Hang them side by side** from the top bar, combs butted so the spacing runs **continuous across the seam** between two cartridges (this seam is the #1 failure mode — watch it). Let the weights settle; nudge any that swing.
6. **Open a shed:** slide the ruler under every-other warp thread across the whole width, turn it on edge → a gap (shed) opens. Pass the weft through. Pull the ruler out, pick up the *opposite* alternates, pass weft back. **Beat** each pass upward snug with the fork.
7. Weave **5–8 cm**. Then look hard.

## The verdict

**PASS** ✅ — weave looks even across the full width; **the seam between two cartridges shows continuous, even spacing** (no fat gap, no crowding); weft packs evenly; you never hand-adjusted tension.
→ The gravity-self-tensioning modular premise holds. Go build the real snap-in cartridge + rigid heddle + crank.

**KILL** ❌ — visible **vertical banding** where cartridges meet (tension mismatch the weights didn't equalize), or a **persistent gap/crowd at the seam** that re-hanging won't fix.
→ The modular premise is in trouble. Stop before spending on snaps or a crank; rethink the cartridge (shared dent module, tongue-and-groove the seam edges) and re-test.

## Expect / remember

- **Low-tension, warp-faced cloth** — gravity gives gentle, constant tension, not a tight balanced weave. That's the fabric class the Smart Loom lives in. Don't judge it against a floor loom.
- **Equal grams-per-thread across cartridges** is non-negotiable — mismatched cartridges band. This becomes a printed spec later ("each cartridge declares its tension").
- **Small bundles** weave more evenly (less intra-thread friction). If 20 ends/cartridge bands, try 12.
- **Swinging weights** = noise. If they sway, rest the pouches lightly against a wall or a slotted guide.
- Total spend should be **under $20**. If you're reaching for more, you're over-building the test.

The point of the weekend: spend a coffee's worth to learn whether the one genuinely new claim survives contact with reality — before a dollar goes to the rest.
